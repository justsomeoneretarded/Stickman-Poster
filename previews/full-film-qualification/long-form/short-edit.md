# Standalone vertical edit plan

Every afternoon, Maya takes the same booth in a rain-streaked café. Across from her is an empty chair. Leo approaches with two cups out of habit, sees the empty seat, and takes one away. Maya watches the place where it almost landed.

She sketches people in her notebook: Leo wrestling with a crooked lamp, Max trying to shelter a paper bag from the rain. She tears the lamp drawing loose and slips it under her saucer. Looking down at the page feels safer than looking at them. When she raises a hand to order, Leo turns toward a crashing tray. When she starts to smile at Max, he looks down at his ringing phone. None of these moments is cruel. Together, they become the story Maya tells herself.

A gust through the opening door scatters Max’s receipts and loose coins. Maya helps him collect them. One receipt is soaked. She wraps his coins in a dry scrap from her sketchbook, leaving a tiny drawing of his ridiculous struggle with the wet bag on the outside. His phone rings again. He mouths a distracted thank-you and rushes out. Maya’s hand stays raised a moment after he has gone.

At her booth, she draws the empty chair again. She tears the page out, almost leaves it on the table, then folds it into her pocket. Leo lifts the lamp drawing from beneath her saucer along with the wet paper scraps nearby. Through the moving tray and cups, she sees what looks like the last of her drawing disappearing into a bin. She leaves before he can catch her eye. Her scarf slips across the seat, hiding the sketchbook she has left behind.

Outside, Maya reaches into her bag and finds no notebook. Through the café window, the empty booth looks like a small stage after everyone has gone home. She almost walks away. Then she spots her crooked-lamp drawing, flattened and taped beside the register. Beneath it sits her notebook, safe and dry.

Inside, Max stands at the counter, holding the paper wrapper she gave him. He is trying to show Leo the drawing, turning it the right way up with the awkward care of someone handling something important. Leo notices Maya and holds up the notebook: “I kept it dry.” Max looks from her to the drawing. “You made this?” Maya nods. For once, she does not immediately lower her eyes.

Leo gestures to a chair beside them while he finishes cleaning the counter. There is a tiny pause in which Maya could retreat to her booth. She pulls the chair out instead. “Can I sit here?” Max shifts his coat out of the way. Leo puts down a cup. Neither makes a speech. Nobody asks her to become a different person.

The café continues around them. Maya opens her sketchbook, then lets the pencil rest. Max is halfway through explaining the ruined paper bag with his hands. Maya looks up and listens. The final wide shot returns to the original booth: the chair across from it is still empty. Maya is simply no longer sitting there.

Ending: Maya puts her pencil down and looks up at the people beside her; return to her old empty booth to complete the visual question.
